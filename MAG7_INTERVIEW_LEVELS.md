Interview Levels at Mag 7:

Statistically and based on broad candidate pass-rate data across Big Tech, here is how the target companies rank from hardest to least hard for Senior/Staff Systems Engineering, Production Engineering, and SRE-Net loops.

1. Google (Hardest)
⚬	Statistical Hardness: ~90–95% failure rate at the Onsite phase.
⚬	Why it's the hardest: Google maintains an unyielding, rigid algorithmic standard. Unlike Meta, Google interviewers rarely pull directly from predictable top-tagged LeetCode lists; they present novel, unseen algorithmic variations.
⚬	The SRE-Net / Systems Friction: In addition to strict algorithms, their Systems & Networking round evaluates theoretical computer science, Linux kernel OS internals, and socket-level edge cases down to the line of code. They also enforce a strict hiring committee consensus process, meaning a single lukewarm interviewer score can derail a packet.

2. Meta (Facebook)
⚬	Statistical Hardness: ~95% total candidate drop-off from initial screen to offer.
⚬	Why it's close behind Google: Meta’s coding bar is defined by extreme speed and zero room for error. You are expected to solve 2 LeetCode Medium/Hard problems in 45 minutes—which leaves under 20 minutes per problem to analyze, write clean Go code, run through test cases, and explain time/space complexity.
⚬	The PE/Systems Friction: For Production Engineering, their Linux Systems & Coding round requires live, bug-free C/Go or Python code that directly manipulates Linux OS primitives under intense time pressure.

3. Amazon / AWS
⚬	Statistical Hardness: ~97% total rejection rate across the entire funnel ("The Loop").
⚬	Why it ranks third: The technical coding bar at Amazon is slightly more standardized (often featuring heavily tagged patterns), but their difficulty lies in the dual bar:
⚬	Bar Raiser Mechanism: An independent interviewer outside the hiring team holds veto power to reject candidates who do not perform better than 50% of current Amazon engineers at that level.
⚬	Behavioral Load: Every single technical round includes 20+ minutes of deep grilling on Amazon’s 16 Leadership Principles. Candidates who write perfect code often fail here due to weak behavioral stories.

4. Apple
⚬	Statistical Hardness: Highly volatile (varies by team, typically ~90% fail rate).
⚬	Why it's in the middle-to-lower tier: Apple does not have a centralized hiring bar or global hiring committee like Google or Meta. Interviews are managed entirely by individual teams (e.g., CoreOS vs. Apple Cloud Services).
⚬	The Systems Friction: If you interview for CoreOS or Low-Level Networking, the technical bar can match or exceed Google's. However, because it is team-dependent, questions focus heavily on real-world practical engineering problems rather than abstract puzzles.

5. Microsoft (Least Hard)
⚬	Statistical Hardness: Comparatively higher pass-through rates than Google or Meta.
⚬	Why it ranks lowest among the 5: Microsoft's technical interview loop is generally more conversational and focused on practical software development.
⚬	The Interview Environment: Algorithms tend to be standard array/string/tree manipulation rather than complex dynamic programming, and system design rounds focus heavily on practical architectural trade-offs rather than abstract theoretical scale.


Summary Rank & Core Strategy
	1.	Google (Hardest: Unseen algorithms + strict hiring committee)
	2.	Meta (Brutal speed: 2 medium/hard problems in 45 minutes)
	3.	Amazon (High behavioral friction + Bar Raiser veto power)
	4.	Apple (Team-dependent: High domain depth, lower algorithmic rigidity)
	5.	Microsoft (Most accessible: Practical systems focus)
